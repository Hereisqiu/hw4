# Design: Campus Customs Storefront

**Concept: "Varsity after dark."** Collegiate block lettering, pennants and polaroid snapshots give it a campus-bookstore feel, set on the project's black-and-pink palette. Pink is used like a team color: it marks every action, price and highlight, so the eye always knows where to go next.

## What I changed, and why

| Area | Change | Why it should make customers stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Typography** | Headings in **Graduate**, a varsity/collegiate slab font, all caps. Body text in **Inter** at 400–900 weights. | It reads as "Yale gear" before a shopper sees a single product. The contrast between display and body fonts creates clear hierarchy: big headline, then a supporting sentence, then the button. |
| **Color system** | Black background, hot pink `#ff4fa3` reserved for **actions and prices only** (buttons, prices, active nav, badges, links), cream white for product photos. | When only clickable things and prices are pink, shoppers scan faster and click more. Prices in pink are easy to compare. White photo panels make the navy and gray garments pop against the dark page. |
| **Announcement ticker** | A scrolling pink strip above the nav: "Officially licensed · 57 Broadway · Game-day tees for Harvard–Yale · Ask Dan…". It pauses on hover. | It mimics real retail banners, quietly adds trust ("officially licensed", a real address), promotes the assistant, and adds life without getting in the way. |
| **Hero** | Two columns. On the left, a varsity headline with an **outlined pink "BULLDOG"** and a pink drop shadow, plus copy and buttons that fade up one after another. On the right, a **collage of real product photos as tilted polaroids** that drop in one by one and straighten on hover, with a **waving pink YALE pennant** on top. | The first screen now shows real merchandise instead of only text: shoppers see hoodies and The Game tee within a second, and every polaroid links to its product. Motion draws the eye to the top-selling items. Two clear buttons: "Shop the collection" and "Ask Dan for help". |
| **Shop by category tiles** | Five image tiles (Hoodies, Crewnecks, Quarter-zips, T-shirts, Fleece & jackets) with "from $X" prices that link straight into the filtered Products page (`/products?category=…`). The image zooms on hover. | It cuts browsing from "scroll 102 items" to one tap. Showing "from $32" lowers the price worry before the click, which helps budget-minded students and gift buyers. |
| **Dan, the CSS bulldog mascot** | A bulldog built entirely from **HTML/CSS shapes** (no image file): ears, brows, eyes with highlights, muzzle, teeth, and a collar with a pink "Y" tag. He appears as the chat launcher (wiggles every few seconds, ears perk up on hover, with an "Ask Dan!" speech bubble), in the chat header (tilts his head while "thinking"), in a home-page banner, and on every product page. The agent prompt was updated so the assistant introduces himself as Dan. | A friendly face makes the assistant approachable. People are much more likely to start a chat with a character than with a generic chat icon, and chatting shoppers find products and sizes faster. Naming him after Yale's mascot ties the assistant to school pride. |
| **"Meet Dan" banner** | A gradient banner with Dan and a **"Find a gift"** button that opens the chat **and asks "What would make a good gift under $40?"** automatically. | It turns gift shoppers (parents, alumni) into a guided path in one click. In testing this showed 25 gift-priced tees on the page immediately. |
| **Product cards** | Staggered fade-up entrance; on hover the card lifts and tilts slightly, the photo zooms, a pink glow appears, and a **"View details →"** pill slides up. Stock badges ("Only 2 left in L") from Problem 9 sit on the photo. | Hover feedback confirms "this is clickable". The zoom lets shoppers inspect graphics without leaving the grid, and the low-stock badges add honest urgency. |
| **Product page** | Large photo with a soft shadow and **zoom on hover**. The info column is **sticky**, so price and sizes stay visible. Size tiles lift on hover; sold-out sizes are crossed out. A dashed **"Questions about this? Ask Dan…"** button opens the chat with this page as context. | Everything needed to decide (price, sizes, stock) stays in view while the shopper looks at the photo. The Ask Dan button catches hesitation ("does it run small?", "is it in pink?") at the moment a shopper might otherwise leave. |
| **Chat experience** | The panel springs open from the corner. Pink gradient header with Dan's face and a status line ("History saved to your account" or "Guest chat"). Speech bubbles have a "tail" corner. New messages slide in. **Bouncing pink typing dots** with "Checking the shelves…" while waiting. Suggestion chips sit above the input. | The chat feels like a polished part of the store, not a bolted-on widget. The typing animation makes the 2–3 second wait feel shorter and signals that Dan is checking real inventory. |
| **Navigation and footer** | Round "CC" monogram badge (rotates on hover), Graduate wordmark, pink underline that slides in under the active or hovered link. A three-column footer with shop shortcuts (Hoodies, Crewnecks, T-shirts) and the store address. | Clear "you are here" signal. The footer catches people who scroll to the bottom and gives them one more path into a category instead of a dead end. |
| **Accessibility and responsiveness** | Visible pink `:focus-visible` outlines. All motion is disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`. On narrow screens the hero stacks, the polaroids shrink, the product info stops being sticky and the footer becomes one column. The ticker pauses on hover. | Shoppers using a keyboard, sensitive to motion, or on a phone (most students) all get a usable store. |

## How this should increase time on site and sales
1. **Faster to a relevant product.** The hero collage, category tiles and footer shortcuts all lead to products in one click, so fewer visitors leave from the home page.
2. **Higher engagement with the assistant.** The mascot, the "Ask Dan" hint, the gift button and the per-product "Ask Dan" button make chatting obvious and inviting. The assistant's answers then put real, in-stock products on the page.
3. **Confidence to buy.** Pink prices, size and stock always visible, honest low-stock badges, "officially licensed" messaging and the store address all reduce uncertainty, the main reason people don't buy.
4. **A memorable brand.** The varsity type, pennant and Dan give Campus Customs a personality that's distinct from generic online stores, which encourages return visits for game days, graduation and gifts.
